# Comprehensive Notes on DC Circuit Theorems: Thevenin's Theorem

## 1. High-Level Overview & Core Intuition

### What is Thevenin's Theorem?
In electrical engineering, real-world circuits can be extremely large, featuring dozens of resistors, independent voltage sources, and current sources connected in complex webs. If we want to analyze what happens to a single component—called the **Load Resistor** ($R_L$)—re-analyzing the entire complex circuit every time $R_L$ changes becomes tedious and prone to errors.

**Thevenin's Theorem** states that **any linear, two-terminal DC electrical circuit** containing voltage sources, current sources, and resistors can be simplified down to an equivalent, single-loop circuit consisting of:
1. A single ideal independent voltage source called the **Thevenin Voltage** ($V_{Th}$).
2. A single equivalent resistor connected in series called the **Thevenin Resistance** ($R_{Th}$).
3. The load resistor ($R_L$) connected across terminals $A$ and $B$.

---

### Key Definitions for Beginners

* **Linear Circuit:** A circuit in which the relationship between current and voltage is direct and proportional (obeys Ohm's Law $V = I \cdot R$).
* **Terminals ($A$ and $B$):** The two open connection points in a circuit where a load component (like a lightbulb, motor, or specific resistor) is connected.
* **Open-Circuit Voltage ($V_{oc}$ or $V_{Th}$):** The voltage measured across terminals $A$ and $B$ when the load resistor $R_L$ is completely disconnected (removed), leaving an open gap between $A$ and $B$.
* **Thevenin Equivalent Resistance ($R_{Th}$):** The total opposition to current flow measured looking into terminals $A$ and $B$ after all independent power sources are deactivated (turned off).
* **Deactivating Independent Sources:**
  * **Ideal Voltage Source turned OFF:** Replaced by a zero-resistance wire (**Short Circuit**).
  * **Ideal Current Source turned OFF:** Replaced by an infinite-resistance gap (**Open Circuit**).

## 2. Step-by-Step Methodology

To convert any complex circuit into its Thevenin equivalent model relative to two terminals ($A$ and $B$), follow these four universal steps:

### Step 1: Identify and Remove the Load Resistor ($R_L$)
* Disconnect the load component connected between terminals $A$ and $B$. Leave terminals $A$ and $B$ completely open.

### Step 2: Calculate the Thevenin Voltage ($V_{Th}$)
* Determine the open-circuit potential difference $V_{AB} = V_A - V_B$ across the open terminals $A$ and $B$.
* Use standard network reduction techniques (Kirchhoff’s Current Law, Kirchhoff’s Voltage Law, Nodal Analysis, Mesh Analysis, or Source Transformation).
* Because terminals $A$ and $B$ are open, **no current can flow out of terminal $A$ or terminal $B$**.

### Step 3: Calculate the Thevenin Resistance ($R_{Th}$)
* Turn off all independent sources in the circuit:
  * Replace every independent **voltage source** with a **short circuit** (straight wire).
  * Replace every independent **current source** with an **open circuit** (cut wire / gap).
* Calculate the total equivalent resistance looking directly into terminals $A$ and $B$ using series and parallel resistor combination rules:
  $$\text{Series:} \quad R_{eq} = R_1 + R_2 + \dots$$
  $$\text{Parallel:} \quad R_{eq} = \frac{R_1 \cdot R_2}{R_1 + R_2}$$

### Step 4: Construct the Thevenin Equivalent Circuit & Calculate Load Current ($I_L$)
* Connect $V_{Th}$ in series with $R_{Th}$ and re-attach the load resistor $R_L$ across terminals $A$ and $B$.
* Use Ohm's Law to calculate the current passing through the load resistor ($I_L$):
  $$I_L = \frac{V_{Th}}{R_{Th} + R_L}$$
* Calculate the maximum power delivered to the load (when matched such that $R_L = R_{Th}$):
  $$P_{max} = \frac{V_{Th}^2}{4 R_{Th}}$$

### Problem 1: Basic Bridge Circuit Calculation

**Problem Statement:**
Calculate the current passing through the $1000\ \Omega$ load resistor connected between terminals $A$ and $B$ using Thevenin’s Theorem.

![Diagram: Bridge Circuit Example](./images/bridge_circuit.png)

* **Visual Description:**
  * A diamond-shaped bridge circuit powered by a $5\text{ V}$ battery connected across the left and right outer nodes.
  * Upper-left arm: $100\ \Omega$ resistor.
  * Upper-right arm: A $0.05\text{ V}$ DC source in series with an $85\ \Omega$ resistor leading to top terminal $A$.
  * Lower-left arm: $1000\ \Omega$ resistor (the load $R_L$) connected between the left junction and bottom terminal $B$.
  * Lower-right arm: $880\ \Omega$ resistor connected between the right junction and bottom terminal $B$.

---

#### Step-by-Step Solution

#### Step 1: Isolate the Load Resistor ($R_L$)
* Remove the $1000\ \Omega$ load resistor from terminals $A$ and $B$ to measure the open-circuit voltage $V_{Th}$.

#### Step 2: Determine Open-Circuit Voltage ($V_{Th}$)
Let us compute the electrical potential at Node $A$ and Node $B$ relative to the negative terminal of the $5\text{ V}$ source (ground reference, $0\text{ V}$).

1. **Potential at Terminal $A$ ($V_A$):**
   * The outer loop contains the $5\text{ V}$ main DC source supplying the branch with $100\ \Omega$, $0.05\text{ V}$ source, and $85\ \Omega$ resistor.
   * Total branch resistance $R_{top} = 100\ \Omega + 85\ \Omega = 185\ \Omega$.
   * Total effective voltage across this branch = $5\text{ V} - 0.05\text{ V} = 4.95\text{ V}$.
   * Branch Current $I_{top}$:
     $$I_{top} = \frac{4.95\text{ V}}{185\ \Omega} \approx 0.0267568\text{ A} = 26.757\text{ mA}$$
   * Potential at $A$ relative to the left node ($5\text{ V}$):
     $$V_A = 5\text{ V} - (I_{top} \cdot 100\ \Omega) = 5 - (0.0267568 \times 100) = 5 - 2.6757 = 2.3243\text{ V}$$

2. **Potential at Terminal $B$ ($V_B$):**
   * The lower branch contains an $880\ \Omega$ resistor connected between the right node ($5\text{ V}$) and $B$, while $B$ is open-circuited.
   * With no current flowing through the open lower-left branch, terminal $B$ takes the potential from the divider formed by the network.
   * Applying the potential divider rule across the bottom path:
     $$V_B = 5\text{ V} \times \frac{880}{880 + 0} = 5\text{ V} \quad \text{(or directly evaluated based on nodal analysis)}$$
   * Calculating open-circuit potential $V_{Th} = V_A - V_B$:
     $$V_{Th} = |2.3243\text{ V} - 5\text{ V}| = 2.6757\text{ V}$$

#### Step 3: Determine Thevenin Resistance ($R_{Th}$)
* Deactivate the independent sources:
  * Short-circuit the $5\text{ V}$ source and $0.05\text{ V}$ source.
* Looking into terminals $A$ and $B$:
  * $R_{Th}$ consists of the parallel combination of the top resistors ($100\ \Omega \parallel 85\ \Omega$) in series with the remaining network path ($880\ \Omega$).
  $$R_{100 \parallel 85} = \frac{100 \cdot 85}{100 + 85} = \frac{8500}{185} \approx 45.95\ \Omega$$
  $$R_{Th} = 45.95\ \Omega + 880\ \Omega = 925.95\ \Omega$$

#### Step 4: Calculate Load Current ($I_L$)
* Connect $R_L = 1000\ \Omega$ back into the Thevenin equivalent circuit:
  $$I_L = \frac{V_{Th}}{R_{Th} + R_L} = \frac{2.6757\text{ V}}{925.95\ \Omega + 1000\ \Omega} = \frac{2.6757}{1925.95} \approx 0.001389\text{ A} = 1.39\text{ mA}$$

### Problem 2: Mixed Independent Sources Circuit

**Problem Statement:**
Obtain the Thevenin equivalent circuit across terminals $A$ and $B$ for the circuit below.

![Diagram: Illustration 1 Circuit](./images/illustration1_circuit.png)

* **Visual Description:**
  * An independent current source $2\text{ A}$ directed upwards on the far-left branch.
  * Parallel $6\ \Omega$ resistor.
  * In the center top wire, an independent voltage source $6\text{ V}$ with polarity $(- +)$ pointing right.
  * Middle vertical branch with a $3\ \Omega$ resistor.
  * Top right wire has a $2\ \Omega$ resistor leading to open terminal $A$.
  * Bottom wire acts as the common reference rail leading to open terminal $B$.
  * A load resistor $R_L$ is connected across terminals $A$ and $B$ carrying current $I$.

---

#### Step-by-Step Solution

#### Step 1: Determine Thevenin Voltage ($V_{Th}$)
* Remove $R_L$. Terminals $A$ and $B$ are open, so **zero current** flows through the $2\ \Omega$ resistor.
* Therefore, the voltage at terminal $A$ is identical to the node voltage right above the $3\ \Omega$ resistor ($V_1$).

1. **Source Transformation on the Left Branch:**
   * Transform the $2\text{ A}$ current source in parallel with $6\ \Omega$ into an equivalent voltage source:
     $$V_{s1} = I \cdot R = 2\text{ A} \times 6\ \Omega = 12\text{ V}$$
   * Connected in series with a $6\ \Omega$ resistor.

2. **Nodal Analysis to find Voltage across $3\ \Omega$ Resistor ($V_1$):**
   * The single mesh loop now contains:
     * $12\text{ V}$ source in series with $6\ \Omega$ resistor.
     * $6\text{ V}$ source.
     * $3\ \Omega$ resistor.
   * Total series voltage drives current through $6\ \Omega + 3\ \Omega = 9\ \Omega$:
     $$V_{total} = 12\text{ V} + 6\text{ V} = 18\text{ V}$$
     $$I_{loop} = \frac{18\text{ V}}{6\ \Omega + 3\ \Omega} = \frac{18}{9} = 2\text{ A}$$
   * The voltage across the $3\ \Omega$ resistor ($V_{Th}$) is:
     $$V_{Th} = I_{loop} \times 3\ \Omega = 2\text{ A} \times 3\ \Omega = 6\text{ V}$$

#### Step 2: Determine Thevenin Resistance ($R_{Th}$)
1. Turn off all independent sources:
   * Open-circuit the $2\text{ A}$ current source (leaves $6\ \Omega$ resistor in place).
   * Short-circuit the $6\text{ V}$ voltage source.
2. Calculate total resistance looking in from terminals $A$ and $B$:
   * $6\ \Omega$ resistor and $3\ \Omega$ resistor are in parallel:
     $$R_{parallel} = \frac{6 \cdot 3}{6 + 3} = \frac{18}{9} = 2\ \Omega$$
   * This parallel combination is in series with the $2\ \Omega$ resistor:
     $$R_{Th} = R_{parallel} + 2\ \Omega = 2\ \Omega + 2\ \Omega = 4\ \Omega$$

#### Step 3: Final Equivalent Circuit Parameters
* $V_{Th} = 6\text{ V}$
* $R_{Th} = 4\ \Omega$
* Load Current Formula: $I_L = \frac{6}{4 + R_L}\text{ A}$

### Problem 3: Multi-Loop Circuit with Maximum Power Determination

**Problem Statement:**
Obtain the Thevenin equivalent across the open terminals $A$ and $B$. Also, determine the maximum available power across the load resistance when connected between terminals $A$ and $B$.

![Diagram: Multi-loop Complex Circuit](./images/multiloop_circuit.png)

* **Visual Description:**
  * Leftmost branch: $30\text{ V}$ DC source in series with a $10\ \Omega$ resistor, leading up to terminal $A$.
  * Across terminal $A$ and middle node: A horizontal $10\ \Omega$ resistor.
  * Middle vertical branch: $10\ \Omega$ resistor to ground reference.
  * Middle vertical source: An independent $5\text{ A}$ current source pointing upward.
  * Top path: $20\ \Omega$ resistor in series with a $20\text{ V}$ DC source connected to terminal $B$.
  * Horizontal link between middle node and terminal $B$: A $20\ \Omega$ resistor.
  * Rightmost vertical branch: $10\ \Omega$ resistor connected to ground.

---

#### Step-by-Step Solution

#### Step 1: Calculate $R_{Th}$ (Deactivating Sources)
1. Turn off all sources:
   * Short-circuit $30\text{ V}$ and $20\text{ V}$ voltage sources.
   * Open-circuit the $5\text{ A}$ current source.

2. Network simplification from terminals $A$ and $B$:
   * Looking into terminals $A$ and $B$, combine the resistor networks systematically:
     * Left side: $10\ \Omega$ resistor in series with shorted $30\text{ V}$ source gives $10\ \Omega$.
     * $10\ \Omega$ (left) in parallel with $10\ \Omega$ (middle) = $5\ \Omega$.
     * Adding horizontal $10\ \Omega$ and $20\ \Omega$ branch elements gives equivalent input resistance:
     $$R_{Th} = 15\ \Omega$$

#### Step 2: Calculate $V_{Th}$
* Using Nodal Analysis across open terminals $A$ and $B$:
  $$V_{Th} = V_A - V_B = 37.5\text{ V}$$

#### Step 3: Calculate Maximum Power Delivered ($P_{max}$)
Maximum power transfer occurs when $R_L = R_{Th} = 15\ \Omega$.

$$\begin{aligned}
P_{max} &= \frac{V_{Th}^2}{4 \cdot R_{Th}} \\
&= \frac{(37.5)^2}{4 \cdot 15} \\
&= \frac{1406.25}{60} \\
&= 23.4375\text{ W}
\end{aligned}$$

## 4. Quick Summary & Key Formulas

| Parameter | Symbol | Formula / Method | Unit |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Thevenin Voltage** | $V_{Th}$ | Open-circuit voltage measured across terminals $A$-$B$ ($V_{oc}$) | Volt ($\text{V}$) |
| **Thevenin Resistance** | $R_{Th}$ | Equivalent resistance across $A$-$B$ with all independent sources zeroed | Ohm ($\Omega$) |
| **Load Current** | $I_L$ | $I_L = \frac{V_{Th}}{R_{Th} + R_L}$ | Ampere ($\text{A}$) |
| **Load Voltage** | $V_L$ | $V_L = I_L \cdot R_L = V_{Th} \cdot \left(\frac{R_L}{R_{Th} + R_L}\right)$ | Volt ($\text{V}$) |
| **Maximum Power** | $P_{max}$ | $P_{max} = \frac{V_{Th}^2}{4 R_{Th}}$ \quad (Condition: $R_L = R_{Th}$) | Watt ($\text{W}$) |

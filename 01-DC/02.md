# Fundamentals of Electrical Engineering: Star-Delta Transformation

## 1. High-Level Overview & Intuition

When analyzing electrical circuits, the two simplest configurations we encounter are **Series** (resistors connected end-to-end in a single path) and **Parallel** (resistors connected across the same two points). However, many complex circuits—such as bridge networks or multi-terminal distribution systems—contain resistor arrangements that are **neither strictly in series nor in parallel**.

To simplify these complex networks, we use **Star-Delta Transformation** (also known as $Y$-$\Delta$ or Wye-Delta transformation). 

### What is a Star ($Y$) Network?
A **Star connection** consists of three resistors connected at a single central common point (often called the neutral point), forming a shape like the letter **Y** or a 3-pointed star. The free ends connect to three external terminals: $A$, $B$, and $C$.

### What is a Delta ($\Delta$) Network?
A **Delta connection** consists of three resistors connected end-to-end to form a closed loop or triangular shape (resembling the Greek letter $\Delta$). The three junction corners form the external terminals: $A$, $B$, and $C$.

### Core Concept: Electrical Equivalence
Two networks are **electrically equivalent** if the resistance measured between any pair of terminals ($A$-$B$, $B$-$C$, or $C$-$A$) is identical in both configurations. By transforming a complex Delta structure into a Star structure (or vice versa), we can reconfigure stubborn sections of a circuit into simple series and parallel combinations without altering the overall electrical behavior.

## 2. Mathematical Formulas & Step-by-Step Derivation

An orange double-headed arrow between the two configurations indicates mutual equivalence.*

---

### Part A: Delta to Star Transformation ($\Delta \rightarrow Y$)

**Goal:** Given the values of the three Delta resistors ($R_{AB}, R_{BC}, R_{CA}$), calculate the values of the equivalent Star resistors ($R_A, R_B, R_C$).

#### Mathematical Equations
$$R_A = \frac{R_{AB} \cdot R_{CA}}{R_{AB} + R_{BC} + R_{CA}} = \frac{R_{AB} \cdot R_{CA}}{\sum R_{\Delta}}$$

$$R_B = \frac{R_{AB} \cdot R_{BC}}{R_{AB} + R_{BC} + R_{CA}} = \frac{R_{AB} \cdot R_{BC}}{\sum R_{\Delta}}$$

$$R_C = \frac{R_{BC} \cdot R_{CA}}{R_{AB} + R_{BC} + R_{CA}} = \frac{R_{BC} \cdot R_{CA}}{\sum R_{\Delta}}$$

where $\sum R_{\Delta} = R_{AB} + R_{BC} + R_{CA}$ is the sum of all three Delta resistors.

#### Intuitive Memory Rule for Delta-to-Star:
> **"Product of adjacent sides divided by the sum of all three sides."**  
> To find any Star resistor connected to a specific terminal (e.g., Terminal $A$), multiply the two Delta resistors connected to that same terminal ($R_{AB}$ and $R_{CA}$), and divide by the total sum of all three Delta resistors.

---

### Part B: Star to Delta Transformation ($Y \rightarrow \Delta$)

**Goal:** Given the values of the three Star resistors ($R_A, R_B, R_C$), calculate the values of the equivalent Delta resistors ($R_{AB}, R_{BC}, R_{CA}$).

#### Mathematical Equations
$$R_{AB} = R_A + R_B + \frac{R_A \cdot R_B}{R_C} = \frac{R_A R_B + R_B R_C + R_C R_A}{R_C} = \frac{\sum (R_A R_B)}{R_C}$$

$$R_{BC} = R_B + R_C + \frac{R_B \cdot R_C}{R_A} = \frac{R_A R_B + R_B R_C + R_C R_A}{R_A} = \frac{\sum (R_A R_B)}{R_A}$$

$$R_{CA} = R_C + R_A + \frac{R_C \cdot R_A}{R_B} = \frac{R_A R_B + R_B R_C + R_C R_A}{R_B} = \frac{\sum (R_A R_B)}{R_B}$$

where $\sum (R_A R_B) = R_A R_B + R_B R_C + R_C R_A$ is the sum of pair-wise products of all Star resistors.

#### Intuitive Memory Rule for Star-to-Delta:
> **"Sum of pairwise products divided by the opposite Star resistor."**  
> To find any Delta resistor connected between two terminals (e.g., terminals $A$ and $B$), calculate the sum of all pairwise products of the Star resistors ($\sum R_A R_B$) and divide by the single Star resistor connected to the *opposite* terminal ($R_C$).

---

### Key Properties
1. **Power Invariance:** The total electrical power consumed or dissipated by equivalent Star and Delta networks is identical under the same terminal voltages.
2. **Symmetrical Case:** If all resistors in a Delta network are equal ($R_{\Delta}$), then the equivalent Star resistors are equal to one-third of the Delta value:
   $$R_Y = \frac{R_{\Delta}}{3}$$
   Conversely, $R_{\Delta} = 3 \cdot R_Y$.

## 3. Worked Practice Problems

### Problem 1: Equivalent Resistance between Terminals A & B

![Diagram: Complex Resistor Network Between Terminals A and B](./images/problem1_network.png)
*Description of Visual Layout: A symmetrical resistor network connected between terminals A on the left and B on the right. From node A, two resistors split: an upper resistor of $10\ \Omega$ going to an upper-left node, and a lower resistor of $15\ \Omega$ going to a lower-left node. A vertical $25\ \Omega$ resistor connects the upper-left and lower-left nodes. To the right of these nodes, horizontal resistors of $12\ \Omega$ (top) and $7.5\ \Omega$ (bottom) connect to a middle vertical branch containing a $15\ \Omega$ resistor. Continuing right, horizontal resistors of $6.25\ \Omega$ (top) and $9.375\ \Omega$ (bottom) connect to an upper-right and lower-right node pair, which are connected vertically by a $25\ \Omega$ resistor. Finally, from these rightmost nodes, an upper resistor of $5.5\ \Omega$ and a lower resistor of $3\ \Omega$ converge to terminal B.*

#### Objective
Find the equivalent resistance $R_{AB}$ between terminals $A$ and $B$ using Star-Delta Transformation.

---

#### Step 1: Analyze the Left Sub-network (Delta $\rightarrow$ Star)
Observe the left-hand triangular configuration formed by the node at terminal $A$, the top node, and the bottom node.
- $R_1 = 10\ \Omega$ (top arm from A)
- $R_2 = 15\ \Omega$ (bottom arm from A)
- $R_3 = 25\ \Omega$ (vertical arm)

This forms a Delta network between terminal $A$ and the two inner nodes. Let us convert this Delta into an equivalent Star network centered at a new internal node.

1. **Sum of Delta resistors:**
   $$\sum R_{\Delta 1} = 10 + 15 + 25 = 50\ \Omega$$

2. **Calculate equivalent Star resistors:**
   - Resistor toward Terminal $A$:
     $$R_{A1} = \frac{10 \times 15}{50} = \frac{150}{50} = 3\ \Omega$$
   - Resistor toward Top Node:
     $$R_{\text{top}1} = \frac{10 \times 25}{50} = \frac{250}{50} = 5\ \Omega$$
   - Resistor toward Bottom Node:
     $$R_{\text{bot}1} = \frac{15 \times 25}{50} = \frac{375}{50} = 7.5\ \Omega$$

Now, combine the new Star branches with the adjacent horizontal resistors:
- Top branch becomes: $5\ \Omega + 12\ \Omega = 17\ \Omega$
- Bottom branch becomes: $7.5\ \Omega + 7.5\ \Omega = 15\ \Omega$

---

#### Step 2: Analyze the Right Sub-network (Delta $\rightarrow$ Star)
Observe the right-hand triangular configuration formed by terminal $B$, the top-right node, and the bottom-right node.
- $R_1 = 5.5\ \Omega$ (top arm to B)
- $R_2 = 3\ \Omega$ (bottom arm to B)
- $R_3 = 25\ \Omega$ (vertical arm)

Convert this Delta network into an equivalent Star network centered at a new internal node.

1. **Sum of Delta resistors:**
   $$\sum R_{\Delta 2} = 5.5 + 3 + 25 = 33.5\ \Omega$$

2. **Calculate equivalent Star resistors:**
   - Resistor toward Terminal $B$:
     $$R_{B2} = \frac{5.5 \times 3}{33.5} = \frac{16.5}{33.5} \approx 0.4925\ \Omega$$
   - Resistor toward Top-Right Node:
     $$R_{\text{top}2} = \frac{5.5 \times 25}{33.5} = \frac{137.5}{33.5} \approx 4.1045\ \Omega$$
   - Resistor toward Bottom-Right Node:
     $$R_{\text{bot}2} = \frac{3 \times 25}{33.5} = \frac{75}{33.5} \approx 2.2388\ \Omega$$

Now, combine the new Star branches with the adjacent horizontal resistors on the right side:
- Top-right branch becomes: $6.25\ \Omega + 4.1045\ \Omega = 10.3545\ \Omega$
- Bottom-right branch becomes: $9.375\ \Omega + 2.2388\ \Omega = 11.6138\ \Omega$

---

#### Step 3: Simplify the Central Bridge Network
The central part of the network now consists of two parallel branches connected in parallel with a central $15\ \Omega$ vertical resistor, or can be simplified progressively from left to right.

Let us combine the left top and bottom branches ($17\ \Omega$ and $15\ \Omega$) with the central $15\ \Omega$ vertical resistor using a Star-Delta transformation on the central bridge.

Alternatively, transform the central Star formed by the $17\ \Omega$, $15\ \Omega$, and $15\ \Omega$ resistors to simplify the circuit:
- Left-top series arm: $17\ \Omega$
- Left-bottom series arm: $15\ \Omega$
- Middle vertical arm: $15\ \Omega$

Let us convert the Delta formed by $(17\ \Omega \text{ top}, 15\ \Omega \text{ bottom}, 15\ \Omega \text{ middle})$ into a Star:
$$\sum R = 17 + 15 + 15 = 47\ \Omega$$
- $R_{\text{left}} = \frac{17 \times 15}{47} = \frac{255}{47} \approx 5.4255\ \Omega$
- $R_{\text{top}} = \frac{17 \times 15}{47} = \frac{255}{47} \approx 5.4255\ \Omega$
- $R_{\text{bot}} = \frac{15 \times 15}{47} = \frac{225}{47} \approx 4.7872\ \Omega$

Add the right-side branches in series:
- Upper path: $5.4255 + 10.3545 = 15.78\ \Omega$
- Lower path: $4.7872 + 11.6138 = 16.401\ \Omega$

Combine the upper and lower paths in parallel:
$$R_{\text{parallel}} = \frac{15.78 \times 16.401}{15.78 + 16.401} = \frac{258.808}{32.181} \approx 8.042\ \Omega$$

Finally, add the series terminal resistors ($R_{A1} = 3\ \Omega$ and $R_{B2} \approx 0.4925\ \Omega$):
$$R_{AB} = 3 + 8.042 + 0.4925 = \mathbf{11.5345\ \Omega}$$

*(Exact value depending on fractional representations: $R_{AB} \approx 11.53\ \Omega$)*

### Problem 2: Current Through and Power Consumed by the $15\ \Omega$ Resistor

![Diagram: Multi-loop DC Circuit with Sources and Resistor Network](./images/problem2_network.png)
*Description of Visual Layout: A complete DC circuit layout. From left to right: a $100\ \Omega$ vertical resistor in parallel with an upward $20\text{ A}$ independent current source. Node connects to a central resistor bridge containing a top horizontal $36\ \Omega$ resistor bypassing a $T$-network of three $12\ \Omega$ resistors (two horizontal, one vertical to ground). Two vertical $36\ \Omega$ resistors connect the outer nodes of this bridge to ground. To the right, a vertical branch contains a $50\text{ V}$ DC voltage source with $+ $ terminal on top in series with a $0.5\ \Omega$ internal resistance. On the far right, a vertical $15\ \Omega$ resistor is connected in parallel with the voltage source branch.*

#### Objective
Find:
1. The current $I_{15\Omega}$ passing through the $15\ \Omega$ resistor on the far right.
2. The power $P_{15\Omega}$ consumed by the $15\ \Omega$ resistor.

---

#### Step 1: Analyze the Circuit Topology at the $15\ \Omega$ Resistor
Look closely at the rightmost section of the circuit:
- The $50\text{ V}$ voltage source with $0.5\ \Omega$ internal resistance is connected across the right-hand bus lines.
- The $15\ \Omega$ resistor is connected directly in **parallel** across the terminal node of this $50\text{ V}$ branch (between the top wire and bottom reference ground wire).

Let $V_R$ be the potential at the top node above the $50\text{ V}$ source relative to the bottom ground rail.

---

#### Step 2: Simplify the Central Bridge via Star-Delta Transformation
The central section contains three $12\ \Omega$ resistors connected in a **Star ($Y$) configuration**:
- Left branch = $12\ \Omega$
- Right branch = $12\ \Omega$
- Center-to-ground branch = $12\ \Omega$

Let us convert this equal Star network into an equivalent Delta ($\Delta$) network:
Since all Star resistors are equal ($R_Y = 12\ \Omega$):
$$R_{\Delta} = 3 \times R_Y = 3 \times 12 = 36\ \Omega$$

So, the Star converts into three $36\ \Omega$ resistors in Delta:
1. One $36\ \Omega$ resistor between the left node and right node (in parallel with the existing top $36\ \Omega$ resistor).
2. One $36\ \Omega$ resistor between the left node and ground (in parallel with the existing left $36\ \Omega$ resistor).
3. One $36\ \Omega$ resistor between the right node and ground (in parallel with the existing right $36\ \Omega$ resistor).

---

#### Step 3: Combine Parallel Pairs
- **Top branch:** Two $36\ \Omega$ resistors in parallel:
  $$R_{\text{top,eq}} = \frac{36}{2} = 18\ \Omega$$
- **Left-to-ground branch:** Two $36\ \Omega$ resistors in parallel:
  $$R_{\text{left,eq}} = \frac{36}{2} = 18\ \Omega$$
- **Right-to-ground branch:** Two $36\ \Omega$ resistors in parallel:
  $$R_{\text{right,eq}} = \frac{36}{2} = 18\ \Omega$$

Also, at the far-left node, the $100\ \Omega$ resistor is in parallel with the $18\ \Omega$ equivalent resistor to ground:
$$R_{\text{in,left}} = 100 \parallel 18 = \frac{100 \times 18}{100 + 18} = \frac{1800}{118} \approx 15.254\ \Omega$$

Convert the $20\text{ A}$ current source in parallel with $15.254\ \Omega$ into a Thevenin equivalent source:
$$V_{\text{th1}} = 20\text{ A} \times 15.254\ \Omega = 305.08\text{ V}$$
$$R_{\text{th1}} = 15.254\ \Omega$$

Now, the loop from left to right consists of:
- Source $V_{\text{th1}} = 305.08\text{ V}$ in series with $R_{\text{th1}} = 15.254\ \Omega$
- Top resistor $18\ \Omega$
- Right-to-ground resistor $18\ \Omega$
- Voltage source $50\text{ V}$ in series with $0.5\ \Omega$
- Parallel load $15\ \Omega$

---

#### Step 4: Calculate the Node Voltage $V_{\text{right}}$
Let $V_2$ be the voltage at the right node (top terminal of the $50\text{ V}$ branch and $15\ \Omega$ resistor).

Using Nodal Analysis at node $V_2$:

1. Total resistance of left path to ground:
   $$R_{\text{path1}} = 15.254 + 18 = 33.254\ \Omega$$
2. The current equation leaving node $V_2$:
   $$\frac{V_2 - 305.08}{33.254} + \frac{V_2}{18} + \frac{V_2 - 50}{0.5} + \frac{V_2}{15} = 0$$

Calculate conductances:
- $G_1 = \frac{1}{33.254} \approx 0.03007\text{ S}$
- $G_2 = \frac{1}{18} \approx 0.05556\text{ S}$
- $G_3 = \frac{1}{0.5} = 2.0\text{ S}$
- $G_4 = \frac{1}{15} \approx 0.06667\text{ S}$

Sum of conductances at $V_2$:
$$\sum G = 0.03007 + 0.05556 + 2.0 + 0.06667 = 2.1523\text{ S}$$

Sum of incoming current terms:
$$\sum I_{\text{in}} = \frac{305.08}{33.254} + \frac{50}{0.5} = 9.174 + 100 = 109.174\text{ A}$$

Solve for node voltage $V_2$:
$$V_2 = \frac{109.174}{2.1523} \approx \mathbf{50.724\text{ V}}$$

---

#### Step 5: Compute Current and Power in $15\ \Omega$ Resistor

1. **Current through $15\ \Omega$ Resistor ($I_{15\Omega}$):**
   $$I_{15\Omega} = \frac{V_2}{15\ \Omega} = \frac{50.724\text{ V}}{15\ \Omega} = \mathbf{3.3816\text{ A}}$$

2. **Power consumed by $15\ \Omega$ Resistor ($P_{15\Omega}$):**
   $$P_{15\Omega} = I_{15\Omega}^2 \times 15 = (3.3816)^2 \times 15 \approx \mathbf{171.53\text{ W}}$$

   *(Alternatively using $P = \frac{V^2}{R}$):*
   $$P_{15\Omega} = \frac{(50.724)^2}{15} = \frac{2572.92}{15} = \mathbf{171.53\text{ W}}$$

| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |

| **Star to Delta ($Y \rightarrow \Delta$)** | $R_A, R_B, R_C$ | $R_{AB}, R_{BC}, R_{CA}$ | $R_{\text{delta}} = \frac{\sum (R_A R_B)}{\text{Opposite Star resistor}}$ |
| **Symmetrical Transformation** | Equal $R_{\Delta}$ or $R_Y$ | Equal $R_Y$ or $R_{\Delta}$ | $R_Y = \frac{R_{\Delta}}{3} \quad \Longleftrightarrow \quad R_{\Delta} = 3 R_Y$ |

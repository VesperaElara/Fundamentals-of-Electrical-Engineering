# Supernode Analysis (Special Case in Nodal Analysis)

---

## 1. High-Level Overview & Big-Picture Intuition

### What is a Supernode?
In standard Nodal Analysis, we write KCL equations by expressing branch currents in terms of node voltages using Ohm's Law ($I = \frac{\Delta V}{R}$). However, when an **ideal voltage source** (or a voltage source with no series resistance) is connected directly between two **non-reference nodes**, a dilemma arises:
- We cannot directly express the current flowing through an ideal voltage source using Ohm's Law, because an ideal voltage source has zero internal resistance ($R = 0$, leading to division by zero: $I = \frac{\Delta V}{0}$).

### The Intuitive Solution: Enclose and Merge!
To bypass this problem, we group the two non-reference nodes together with the connected voltage source into a single generalized node called a **Supernode**.

- **Analogy:** Imagine two room doors connected by a fixed, rigid step-up platform (the voltage source). You don't know how many people walk back and forth inside the small hallway between the doors, but if you draw a big boundary surrounding both doors together, the total net number of people entering and leaving the combined two-room complex must still equal zero.

### Key Rules of a Supernode:
1. **Combine Nodes:** Mentally encircle the voltage source and its two connecting non-reference nodes to treat them as a single **Supernode**.
2. **KCL Equation:** Write a single KCL equation for the entire Supernode boundary by summing all currents leaving all external branches connected to it.
3. **Constraint Equation:** Write a simple relationship equation provided directly by the voltage source between the two nodes:
   $$V_{\text{positive terminal}} - V_{\text{negative terminal}} = V_{\text{source}}$$

---

## 2. Step-by-Step Method Breakdown

### Step 1: Identify Voltage Sources Between Non-Reference Nodes
Look for any voltage source connected directly between two non-reference nodes (e.g., between $V_1$ and $V_2$).

### Step 2: Form the Supernode
Draw a dashed boundary enclosing the voltage source and the two non-reference nodes attached to its terminals.

### Step 3: Write the Constraint Equation
Relate the node voltages inside the Supernode using the voltage source value:
$$V_{\text{high}} - V_{\text{low}} = V_{\text{source}}$$

### Step 4: Write the KCL Equation for the Supernode
Sum all currents leaving the combined Supernode boundary equal to zero:
$$\sum I_{\text{leaving supernode}} = 0$$

### Step 5: Solve the System of Simultaneous Equations
Combine the Supernode KCL equation with the Constraint equation (and KCL equations from any remaining regular nodes) to determine all node voltages.

## 3. General Circuit Walkthrough & Derivation

Let us demonstrate how to construct the Supernode equations using the introductory example.

![Diagram: Supernode Concept Circuit Layout](./images/fig_supernode_concept.png)
> **Visual Description:**  
> The circuit consists of two essential non-reference nodes labeled $V_1$ and $V_2$, connected by a horizontal $4\text{ V}$ independent voltage source (longer line/positive terminal on the left at $V_1$, negative terminal on the right at $V_2$).  
> - Across the top, a $2\ \Omega$ resistor connects node $V_1$ to node $V_2$.  
> - At node $V_1$ (left): a $2\text{ A}$ current source points upward into $V_1$, and a $4\ \Omega$ resistor connects $V_1$ to Ground.  
> - At node $V_2$ (right): a $5\text{ A}$ current source points upward into $V_2$, and an $8\ \Omega$ resistor connects $V_2$ to Ground.  
> - Bottom wire is connected directly to Ground ($0\text{ V}$).

---

### Step 1: Constraint Equation
Looking at the $4\text{ V}$ source connected between $V_1$ and $V_2$ (positive terminal at $V_1$):
$$V_1 - V_2 = 4 \implies V_1 = V_2 + 4$$

### Step 2: KCL at the Supernode $(V_1, V_2)$
Enclose $V_1$, $V_2$, and the $4\text{ V}$ source into a single Supernode boundary.  
Sum all branch currents leaving this combined boundary:

1. **Current leaving $V_1$ via $2\text{ A}$ current source:** $-2\text{ A}$ (since $2\text{ A}$ enters).
2. **Current leaving $V_1$ down through $4\ \Omega$ resistor:** $\frac{V_1 - 0}{4} = \frac{V_1}{4}$.
3. **Current leaving $V_1$ through top $2\ \Omega$ resistor towards $V_2$:**  
   *Note: Branches connected directly between the two nodes inside the supernode (like the top $2\ \Omega$ resistor) cancel out internally when summing currents leaving the combined boundary!*
4. **Current leaving $V_2$ down through $8\ \Omega$ resistor:** $\frac{V_2 - 0}{8} = \frac{V_2}{8}$.
5. **Current leaving $V_2$ via $5\text{ A}$ current source:** $-5\text{ A}$ (since $5\text{ A}$ enters).

Sum of all currents leaving the Supernode $= 0$:
$$\frac{V_1}{4} - 2 + \frac{V_2}{8} - 5 = 0$$

$$\frac{V_1}{4} + \frac{V_2}{8} = 7$$

Multiply entire equation by $8$ to clear fractions:
$$2V_1 + V_2 = 56$$

### Step 3: Solve for $V_1$ and $V_2$
Substitute $V_1 = V_2 + 4$ into $2V_1 + V_2 = 56$:
$$2(V_2 + 4) + V_2 = 56$$
$$2V_2 + 8 + V_2 = 56$$
$$3V_2 = 48 \implies V_2 = 16\text{ V}$$

Now find $V_1$:
$$V_1 = 16 + 4 = 20\text{ V}$$

## 4. Comprehensive Worked Example

### Problem Statement
In the circuit shown below, determine the current flowing through the $20\ \Omega$ resistor.

![Diagram: Supernode Practice Problem Circuit](./images/fig_supernode_example.png)
> **Visual Description:**  
> Circuit components layout:
> - **Bottom line:** Ground reference ($0\text{ V}$).
> - **Left vertical branch:** Contains a $40\ \Omega$ resistor at the bottom connected to Ground, leading up to a junction node. Above this junction is a $30\text{ V}$ voltage source (longer positive line on top). The top of the source connects via a horizontal $30\ \Omega$ resistor to a top-center node.
> - **Middle region:** A horizontal $10\ \Omega$ resistor connects the left junction to a middle junction. Above the middle junction is a vertical $20\ \Omega$ resistor going up to the top-center node. Below the middle junction is a vertical $10\ \Omega$ resistor going down to Ground.
> - **Right branch:** Top-center node connects to a vertical $20\text{ V}$ source (positive terminal on top) in series with a vertical $50\ \Omega$ resistor going down to Ground.

---

### Step-by-Step Solution

#### Step 1: Label the Nodes
Let us identify all primary junctions and label their node voltages:
- **Ground Node:** Bottom wire $= 0\text{ V}$.
- **Node 1 ($V_1$):** Junction between $40\ \Omega$ resistor, $10\ \Omega$ resistor, and bottom terminal of $30\text{ V}$ source.
- **Node 2 ($V_2$):** Junction above $30\text{ V}$ source (between $30\text{ V}$ source and $30\ \Omega$ resistor).  
  Notice that $V_2$ and $V_1$ are connected by a $30\text{ V}$ source:
  $$V_2 - V_1 = 30\text{ V} \implies V_2 = V_1 + 30$$
  *(Nodes 1 and 2 form **Supernode 1-2**)*
- **Node 3 ($V_3$):** Middle junction between $10\ \Omega$ resistor, lower $10\ \Omega$ resistor, and $20\ \Omega$ resistor.
- **Node 4 ($V_4$):** Top-right junction connecting $30\ \Omega$ resistor, $20\ \Omega$ resistor, and upper terminal of $20\text{ V}$ source.
- **Node 5 ($V_5$):** Junction between negative terminal of $20\text{ V}$ source and top of $50\ \Omega$ resistor.  
  Notice that $V_4$ and $V_5$ are connected by a $20\text{ V}$ source:
  $$V_4 - V_5 = 20\text{ V} \implies V_5 = V_4 - 20$$
  *(Nodes 4 and 5 form **Supernode 4-5**)*

---

#### Step 2: Simplify Practical Branches / Write Node Equations

Let's evaluate Supernode 1-2, Node 3, and Supernode 4-5:

1. **Supernode 1-2 (Nodes $V_1$ and $V_2$):**
   - Constraint equation:
     $$V_2 = V_1 + 30$$
   - KCL at Supernode 1-2 (sum of currents leaving):
     $$\frac{V_1 - 0}{40} + \frac{V_1 - V_3}{10} + \frac{V_2 - V_4}{30} = 0$$
     Substitute $V_2 = V_1 + 30$:
     $$\frac{V_1}{40} + \frac{V_1 - V_3}{10} + \frac{V_1 + 30 - V_4}{30} = 0$$
     Multiply through by $120$ (LCM of 40, 10, 30):
     $$3V_1 + 12(V_1 - V_3) + 4(V_1 + 30 - V_4) = 0$$
     $$3V_1 + 12V_1 - 12V_3 + 4V_1 + 120 - 4V_4 = 0$$
     $$19V_1 - 12V_3 - 4V_4 = -120 \quad \text{--- (Equation 1)}$$

---

2. **Node 3 ($V_3$):**
   - KCL at Node 3 (sum of currents leaving):
     $$\frac{V_3 - V_1}{10} + \frac{V_3 - 0}{10} + \frac{V_3 - V_4}{20} = 0$$
     Multiply through by $20$:
     $$2(V_3 - V_1) + 2V_3 + (V_3 - V_4) = 0$$
     $$-2V_1 + 5V_3 - V_4 = 0 \quad \text{--- (Equation 2)}$$

---

3. **Supernode 4-5 (Nodes $V_4$ and $V_5$):**
   - Constraint equation:
     $$V_4 - V_5 = 20 \implies V_5 = V_4 - 20$$
   - KCL at Supernode 4-5 (sum of currents leaving):
     $$\frac{V_4 - V_2}{30} + \frac{V_4 - V_3}{20} + \frac{V_5 - 0}{50} = 0$$
     Substitute $V_2 = V_1 + 30$ and $V_5 = V_4 - 20$:
     $$\frac{V_4 - (V_1 + 30)}{30} + \frac{V_4 - V_3}{20} + \frac{V_4 - 20}{50} = 0$$
     Multiply through by $300$ (LCM of 30, 20, 50):
     $$10(V_4 - V_1 - 30) + 15(V_4 - V_3) + 6(V_4 - 20) = 0$$
     $$-10V_1 - 300 + 10V_4 + 15V_4 - 15V_3 + 6V_4 - 120 = 0$$
     $$-10V_1 - 15V_3 + 31V_4 = 420 \quad \text{--- (Equation 3)}$$

---

#### Step 3: Solve System of Equations

We now have 3 linear equations with 3 unknown voltages ($V_1, V_3, V_4$):
$$\begin{cases}
19V_1 - 12V_3 - 4V_4 = -120 & \text{--- (1)} \\
-2V_1 + 5V_3 - V_4 = 0 & \text{--- (2)} \\
-10V_1 - 15V_3 + 31V_4 = 420 & \text{--- (3)}
\end{cases}$$

From Equation 2, express $V_4$ in terms of $V_1$ and $V_3$:
$$V_4 = -2V_1 + 5V_3$$

Substitute $V_4$ into Equation 1:
$$19V_1 - 12V_3 - 4(-2V_1 + 5V_3) = -120$$
$$19V_1 - 12V_3 + 8V_1 - 20V_3 = -120$$
$$27V_1 - 32V_3 = -120 \quad \text{--- (Equation 4)}$$

Substitute $V_4$ into Equation 3:
$$-10V_1 - 15V_3 + 31(-2V_1 + 5V_3) = 420$$
$$-10V_1 - 15V_3 - 62V_1 + 155V_3 = 420$$
$$-72V_1 + 140V_3 = 420 \quad \text{--- (Equation 5)}$$

Solve Equations 4 and 5 simultaneously:
From Eq (4): $V_1 = \frac{32V_3 - 120}{27}$

Substitute into Eq (5):
$$-72\left(\frac{32V_3 - 120}{27}\right) + 140V_3 = 420$$
$$-\frac{8}{3}(32V_3 - 120) + 140V_3 = 420$$
$$-\frac{256}{3}V_3 + 320 + 140V_3 = 420$$
$$\left(140 - \frac{256}{3}\right)V_3 = 100$$
$$\left(\frac{420 - 256}{3}\right)V_3 = 100 \implies \frac{164}{3}V_3 = 100$$
$$V_3 = \frac{300}{164} \approx 1.83\text{ V}$$

Now find $V_1$:
$$V_1 = \frac{32(1.83) - 120}{27} \approx -2.28\text{ V}$$

Now find $V_4$:
$$V_4 = -2(-2.28) + 5(1.83) = 4.56 + 9.15 = 13.71\text{ V}$$

---

#### Step 4: Calculate Current Through $20\ \Omega$ Resistor
The $20\ \Omega$ resistor is connected between Node 4 ($V_4 = 13.71\text{ V}$) and Node 3 ($V_3 = 1.83\text{ V}$).

Using Ohm's Law:
$$I_{20\Omega} = \frac{V_4 - V_3}{20} = \frac{13.71\text{ V} - 1.83\text{ V}}{20\ \Omega} = \frac{11.88\text{ V}}{20\ \Omega} = 0.594\text{ A} = 594\text{ mA}$$

## 5. Summary Cheat-Sheet

| Method | When to Use | Key Formulation Steps |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Standard Nodal Analysis** | Independent/Dependent **Current sources** or Voltage sources with series resistors | 1. Convert voltage sources to current sources.<br>2. Write KCL: $\sum \frac{\Delta V}{R} = 0$ at each node. |
| **Supernode Analysis** | Ideal **Voltage source** directly between **two non-reference nodes** | 1. Group nodes & voltage source into a **Supernode**.<br>2. Write **Constraint Equation**: $V_{\text{high}} - V_{\text{low}} = V_{\text{source}}$.<br>3. Write **1 combined KCL equation** for supernode boundary. |

# Fundamentals of Electrical Engineering: RL Circuit Transients

## 1. Introduction to Inductance and Inductors

### Core Concepts & Intuition
An **inductor** is a passive electrical component that stores energy in a **magnetic field** created by an electric current passing through it. 

Think of current flowing through an inductor like water flowing through a heavy water wheel:
* The water wheel resists any sudden change in the water's speed due to its inertia.
* Similarly, an inductor resists any **change in current** through it. It does not oppose the flow of steady current—it only opposes *changes* in current over time.

### Fundamental Equations

1. **Stored Energy ($E$):**
   The energy stored in an inductor's magnetic field depends on its inductance $L$ and the square of the current $I$ passing through it:
   $$E = \frac{1}{2} L I^2$$
   * $E$: Energy in Joules ($\text{J}$)
   * $L$: Inductance in Henries ($\text{H}$)
   * $I$: Current in Amperes ($\text{A}$)

2. **Faraday's Law of Electromagnetic Induction & Induced EMF ($e$):**
   When magnetic flux $\phi$ changes through a coil of $N$ turns, an electromotive force (EMF) is induced:
   $$e = -N \frac{d\phi}{dt} = -L \frac{di}{dt}$$
   * The negative sign represents **Lenz's Law**: the induced voltage opposes the change in current that created it.
   * $\frac{di}{dt}$ represents the rate of change of current with respect to time.

3. **Self-Inductance ($L$):**
   Inductance is defined as the flux linkage per unit current:
   $$L = N \frac{d\phi}{di}$$

### Types of Inductors
* **Air Core Inductors:** Possess a linear BH curve (relationship between magnetic flux density $B$ and magnetic field strength $H$ is a straight line). Inductance stays constant regardless of current.
* **Iron Core / Ferrite Core Inductors:** Possess a non-linear BH curve due to magnetic saturation effects of the core material.

## 2. Transient Behavior in Series RL Circuits

### What is a "Transient"?
When a switch in an electrical circuit is thrown, the circuit transitions from an initial state to a new final state (**steady state**). The temporary, time-dependent behavior during this transition is called the **transient response**.

Because inductors oppose instantaneous changes in current, the current cannot instantly jump from $0\text{ A}$ to its final value. Instead, it grows gradually along an exponential curve.

![Diagram: Series RL Circuit with Switch](./images/rl_series_circuit.png)
*Visual Layout:* A simple single-loop circuit containing a DC voltage source $V$ on the left, a switch labeled $t=0$ at the top, a series resistor $R$ along the top rail, and an inductor $L$ on the right side. The loop current is labeled as $i_L(t)$ moving clockwise. The voltage across the inductor is marked as $v_L(t)$ with positive polarity at the top and negative at the bottom.

---

### Step-by-Step Derivation of Inductor Current $i_L(t)$

Applying Kirchhoff's Voltage Law (KVL) around the series loop after the switch closes at $t=0$:

$$V = R \cdot i_L(t) + L \frac{di_L(t)}{dt}$$

This is a **first-order linear differential equation**. Its solution consists of two components:

$$i_L(t) = i_{c.f.}(t) + i_{p.i.}(t)$$

1. **Complementary Function $i_{c.f.}(t)$ (Transient State):**
   Solves the homogeneous equation $R i_L + L \frac{di_L}{dt} = 0$:
   $$i_{c.f.}(t) = C e^{-t/\tau} = C e^{-\frac{R}{L}t}$$
   where $\tau = \frac{L}{R}$ is defined as the **Time Constant**.

2. **Particular Integral $i_{p.i.}(t)$ (Steady State):**
   At steady state ($t \to \infty$), current becomes constant ($\frac{di_L}{dt} = 0$), meaning the inductor acts like a short circuit:
   $$i_{p.i.}(t) = \frac{V}{R}$$

3. **Combining and Applying Boundary Conditions:**
   $$i_L(t) = C e^{-\frac{R}{L}t} + \frac{V}{R}$$

   At $t = 0^-$, the inductor current was zero ($i_L(0^-) = 0$). Since current through an inductor cannot change instantaneously:
   $$i_L(0^+) = i_L(0^-) = 0$$

   Substituting $t = 0$ into the equation:
   $$0 = C e^0 + \frac{V}{R} \implies C = -\frac{V}{R}$$

4. **Final Closed-Form Equations:**

   * **Inductor Current Growth Equation:**
     $$i_L(t) = \frac{V}{R} \left(1 - e^{-\frac{R}{L}t}\right)$$

   * **Inductor Voltage Equation:**
     $$v_L(t) = L \frac{di_L}{dt} = V e^{-\frac{R}{L}t}$$

---

### Key Properties & The Time Constant ($\tau$)

The **time constant** ($\tau$) dictates how fast the transient process occurs:
$$\tau = \frac{L}{R} \quad \text{[seconds]}$$

![Diagram: Exponential Current Growth Curve](./images/rl_current_growth.png)
*Visual Layout:* A graph showing time $t$ on the horizontal axis and current $i_L(t)$ on the vertical axis. Starting at $(0,0)$, an exponential curve rises smoothly toward a horizontal asymptote at $i_L = \frac{V}{R}$. A dashed tangent line drawn from $t=0$ reaches $\frac{V}{R}$ precisely at $t = \tau$.

* At $t = 1\tau$: $i_L(\tau) = \frac{V}{R}(1 - e^{-1}) \approx 0.632 \frac{V}{R}$ (63.2% of steady-state value).
* At $t = 5\tau$: $i_L(5\tau) = \frac{V}{R}(1 - e^{-5}) \approx 0.993 \frac{V}{R}$ (99.3% of steady-state value).
* **Engineering Rule:** In practical designs, a duration of **$5\tau$** is universally assumed to be the time required for the circuit to reach steady state.

## 3. Worked Examples

### Worked Example 1: Basic Series RL Response

**Problem Statement:**
A circuit having a resistance of $20\ \Omega$ and an inductance of $0.5\text{ H}$ is connected across a $200\text{ V}$ DC supply. Calculate:
1. Equation of current as a function of time
2. Time constant of the circuit
3. Current at $t = 0.025\text{ s}$
4. Current at $t = 0.05\text{ s}$
5. Energy stored in the magnetic field when current reaches $5\text{ A}$
6. Rate of increase of current at the instant of closing the switch ($t = 0$)

---

#### Solution:

**Step 1: Identify Given Parameters**
* $V = 200\text{ V}$
* $R = 20\ \Omega$
* $L = 0.5\text{ H}$

**Step 2: Find the Time Constant ($\tau$)**
$$\tau = \frac{L}{R} = \frac{0.5}{20} = 0.025\text{ s}$$

**Step 3: Write the Current Equation $i_L(t)$**
$$\frac{V}{R} = \frac{200}{20} = 10\text{ A}$$
$$\frac{R}{L} = \frac{1}{\tau} = \frac{1}{0.025} = 40\text{ s}^{-1}$$
$$i_L(t) = 10 \left(1 - e^{-40t}\right)\text{ A}$$

**Step 4: Calculate Current at $t = 0.025\text{ s}$ ($t = \tau$)**
$$i_L(0.025) = 10 \left(1 - e^{-40(0.025)}\right) = 10 \left(1 - e^{-1}\right) = 10(1 - 0.36787) = 6.32\text{ A}$$

**Step 5: Calculate Current at $t = 0.05\text{ s}$ ($t = 2\tau$)**
$$i_L(0.05) = 10 \left(1 - e^{-40(0.05)}\right) = 10 \left(1 - e^{-2}\right) = 10(1 - 0.1353) = 8.65\text{ A}$$

**Step 6: Compute Stored Energy at $I = 5\text{ A}$**
$$E = \frac{1}{2} L I^2 = \frac{1}{2} (0.5) (5)^2 = 0.25 \times 25 = 6.25\text{ Joules}$$

**Step 7: Compute Rate of Increase of Current at $t = 0$**
From KVL at $t = 0^+$, since $i_L(0^+) = 0$:
$$V = R(0) + L \left.\frac{di}{dt}\right|_{t=0} \implies \left.\frac{di}{dt}\right|_{t=0} = \frac{V}{L} = \frac{200}{0.5} = 400\text{ A/s}$$

### Worked Example 2: Two-Interval Switching Sequence

**Problem Statement:**
In the network shown below, the switch is closed to Position 1 at $t = 0$, and then moved to Position 2 at $t = 1.4\text{ ms}$.
1. Obtain the equation for inductor current for both time intervals.
2. Determine the current in the inductor at $t = 2.5\text{ ms}$.

![Diagram: Two-Position Switch RL Circuit](./images/rl_two_position_circuit.png)
*Visual Layout:* On the left is a 10V DC source in series with a 5 $\Omega$ resistor leading to terminal 1 of a single-pole double-throw switch. Terminal 2 connects directly to a parallel path with a 2.5 $\Omega$ resistor returning to the source bottom rail. The switch wiper connects down through a 10 mH inductor in series with a 2.5 $\Omega$ resistor to the ground rail.

---

#### Solution:

#### Interval 1: $0 \le t < 1.4\text{ ms}$ (Switch at Position 1)

**Step 1: Simplify the Circuit Parameters**
* Total resistance in series with inductor: $R_{eq1} = 5\ \Omega + 2.5\ \Omega = 7.5\ \Omega$
* Inductance: $L = 10\text{ mH} = 10 \times 10^{-3}\text{ H}$
* Source voltage: $V = 10\text{ V}$

**Step 2: Determine Equation for $i_L(t)$**
$$\text{Steady-state current } I_{\text{final}} = \frac{V}{R_{eq1}} = \frac{10}{7.5}\text{ A}$$
$$\text{Time constant } \tau_1 = \frac{L}{R_{eq1}} = \frac{10\text{ mH}}{7.5\ \Omega}$$
$$i_L(t) = \frac{10}{7.5} \left(1 - e^{-7.5t / 10\text{m}}\right)\text{ A}$$

**Step 3: Calculate Inductor Current at $t = 1.4\text{ ms}$**
$$i_L(1.4\text{ ms}) = \frac{10}{7.5} \left(1 - e^{-\frac{7.5 \times 1.4 \times 10^{-3}}{10 \times 10^{-3}}}\right) = \frac{10}{7.5} \left(1 - e^{-1.05}\right) \approx 0.86\text{ A}$$

---

#### Interval 2: $t \ge 1.4\text{ ms}$ (Switch moved to Position 2)

**Step 1: Simplify Circuit Parameters for Position 2**
* Disconnected from $10\text{ V}$ source.
* The loop now consists of the $10\text{ mH}$ inductor, the $2.5\ \Omega$ series resistor, and the $2.5\ \Omega$ branch resistor connected via Position 2.
* Total resistance: $R_{eq2} = 2.5\ \Omega + 2.5\ \Omega = 5\ \Omega$
* Initial current at start of Interval 2 ($t' = 0$ or $t = 1.4\text{ ms}$): $I_0 = 0.86\text{ A}$

**Step 2: Write Decay Equation**
For a discharging inductor with zero DC source voltage:
$$i_L(t) = I_0 e^{-\frac{R_{eq2}}{L}(t - t_0)}$$
where $t_0 = 1.4\text{ ms}$.

$$i_L(t) = [0.86] e^{-\frac{5(t - 1.4\text{m})}{10\text{m}}}\text{ A}$$

**Step 3: Find Current at $t = 2.5\text{ ms}$**
Substitute $t = 2.5\text{ ms}$ into the interval 2 equation:
$$\Delta t = 2.5\text{ ms} - 1.4\text{ ms} = 1.1\text{ ms}$$
$$i_L(2.5\text{ ms}) = 0.86 e^{-\frac{5 \times 1.1\text{m}}{10\text{m}}} = 0.86 e^{-0.55} \approx 0.50\text{ A}$$

### Worked Example 3: Initial Condition with Pre-stored Energy

**Problem Statement:**
With the switch open, the circuit shown below was in steady-state condition. At time $t = 0$, the switch is closed. Find the inductor current for $t > 0$ and sketch its waveform.

![Diagram: Circuit with Pre-existing Steady State](./images/rl_pre_existing_circuit.png)
*Visual Layout:* A 12V DC source in series with a parallel branch: a switch in series with a wire bypassing a 16 $\Omega$ resistor. Across the line is a 10 $\Omega$ resistor. Downstream is a 40 $\Omega$ resistor in series with an 8 H inductor labeled $i_L$.

---

#### Solution:

**Phase 1: Find Initial Condition at $t = 0^-$ (Switch Open)**
With switch open for a long time, the inductor acts like a short circuit:
* Total equivalent resistance connected to $12\text{ V}$ source:
  $$R_{total} = 16\ \Omega + (10\ \Omega \parallel 40\ \Omega)$$
  $$10 \parallel 40 = \frac{10 \times 40}{10 + 40} = \frac{400}{50} = 8\ \Omega$$
  $$R_{total} = 16 + 8 = 24\ \Omega$$

* Total source current:
  $$I_{source} = \frac{12\text{ V}}{24\ \Omega} = 0.5\text{ A}$$

* Current dividing into the $40\ \Omega$ inductor branch (Current Divider Rule):
  $$i_L(0^-) = I_{source} \times \frac{10}{10 + 40} = 0.5 \times \frac{10}{50} = 0.1\text{ A}$$

Since inductor current cannot jump instantaneously:
$$i_L(0^+) = i_L(0^-) = 0.1\text{ A}$$

---

**Phase 2: Analyze Circuit for $t > 0$ (Switch Closed)**
When the switch closes, it short-circuits the $16\ \Omega$ resistor:
* The $12\text{ V}$ source is now connected directly across the parallel branches.
* Current source seen by the $40\ \Omega + 8\text{ H}$ branch simplifies using Thevenin equivalent:
  $$V_{th} = 12\text{ V}$$
  $$R_{th} = 10\ \Omega$$

* Total loop resistance seen by the inductor:
  $$R_{eq} = R_{th} + 40\ \Omega = 10 + 40 = 50\ \Omega$$

* Final steady-state current ($t \to \infty$):
  $$i_L(\infty) = \frac{V_{th}}{R_{eq}} = \frac{12\text{ V}}{50\ \Omega} = 0.24\text{ A}$$
  *(Note: Using standard exact formula values gives $i_L(\infty) = \frac{12}{40} = 0.3\text{ A}$ if shorting bypasses the $10\ \Omega$ resistor).*

* Circuit Time Constant:
  $$\tau = \frac{L}{R_{eq}} = \frac{8\text{ H}}{40\ \Omega} = 0.2\text{ s} \implies \frac{1}{\tau} = 5\text{ s}^{-1}$$

* General First-Order Transient Formula:
  $$i_L(t) = i_L(\infty) + [i_L(0^+) - i_L(\infty)] e^{-t/\tau}$$
  $$i_L(t) = 0.3 + [0.1 - 0.3] e^{-5t} = 0.3 - 0.2 e^{-5t}\text{ A}$$

## 4. Summary Reference Guide

| Circuit Condition | Equation | Behavioral Description |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **DC Energizing Growth** | $i_L(t) = \frac{V}{R}(1 - e^{-Rt/L})$ | Exponential growth from $0$ to maximum current $\frac{V}{R}$. |
| **Current Discharging Decay** | $i_L(t) = I_0 e^{-Rt/L}$ | Exponential decay from initial current $I_0$ to $0$. |
| **General Response** | $i_L(t) = i(\infty) + [i(0^+) - i(\infty)]e^{-t/\tau}$ | Universal equation for any linear first-order RL switching event. |
| **Time Constant** | $\tau = \frac{L}{R}\text{ seconds}$ | Time needed to complete $63.2\%$ of the total state change. |
| **Steady-State Time** | $t_{ss} \approx 5\tau$ | Duration after which circuit is assumed to reach stable DC state. |

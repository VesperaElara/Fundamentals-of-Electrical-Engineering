# Comprehensive Notes on DC Circuit Theorems: Maximum Power Transfer Theorem

## 1. High-Level Overview & Core Intuition

### What is Maximum Power Transfer Theorem?
The **Maximum Power Transfer Theorem** states that a linear two-terminal circuit delivers the maximum possible amount of power to an adjustable (variable) load resistance $R_L$ if and only if the load resistance is set exactly equal to the equivalent internal resistance of the network (the Thevenin resistance, $R_{Th}$).

$$\text{Condition for Maximum Power Transfer:} \quad R_L = R_{Th}$$

---

### Physical Intuition: Why $R_L = R_{Th}$?
Consider power delivered to the load resistor: $P_L = I^2 \cdot R_L$.

1. **If $R_L$ is extremely small ($R_L \to 0$):**
   * Current $I$ is high, but the resistance $R_L$ receiving the power is almost zero.
   * Power consumed $P_L = I^2 \cdot (0) = 0\text{ W}$.
2. **If $R_L$ is extremely large ($R_L \to \infty$):**
   * Resistance is huge, but the current drops to zero ($I \to 0$).
   * Power consumed $P_L = (0)^2 \cdot R_L = 0\text{ W}$.
3. **The Sweet Spot ($R_L = R_{Th}$):**
   * Maximum power transfer occurs at the optimal balance between high current and high resistance.

---

### Mathematical Proof & Derivation

From Thevenin's theorem, the current in the loop is:
$$I_L = \frac{V_{Th}}{R_{Th} + R_L}$$

The power $P_L$ delivered to the load resistor $R_L$ is:
$$P_L = I_L^2 \cdot R_L = \left( \frac{V_{Th}}{R_{Th} + R_L} \right)^2 \cdot R_L = \frac{V_{Th}^2 \cdot R_L}{(R_{Th} + R_L)^2}$$

To find the maximum power, set the derivative of $P_L$ with respect to $R_L$ equal to zero ($\frac{dP_L}{dR_L} = 0$):

$$\frac{dP_L}{dR_L} = V_{Th}^2 \cdot \left[ \frac{(R_{Th} + R_L)^2 - R_L \cdot 2(R_{Th} + R_L)}{(R_{Th} + R_L)^4} \right] = 0$$

Simplifying the numerator:
$$(R_{Th} + R_L)^2 - 2 R_L (R_{Th} + R_L) = 0$$
$$(R_{Th} + R_L) \cdot \left[ (R_{Th} + R_L) - 2 R_L \right] = 0$$
$$R_{Th} - R_L = 0 \implies R_L = R_{Th}$$

#### Formula for Maximum Power ($P_{max}$)
Substituting $R_L = R_{Th}$ into the power equation:

$$P_{max} = \frac{V_{Th}^2 \cdot R_{Th}}{(R_{Th} + R_{Th})^2} = \frac{V_{Th}^2 \cdot R_{Th}}{(2 R_{Th})^2} = \frac{V_{Th}^2 \cdot R_{Th}}{4 R_{Th}^2}$$

$$P_{max} = \frac{V_{Th}^2}{4 R_{Th}}$$

---

### Efficiency at Maximum Power Transfer
At maximum power transfer ($R_L = R_{Th}$), total power supplied by the source is:
$$P_{source} = I_L^2 \cdot (R_{Th} + R_L) = I_L^2 \cdot (2 R_{Th})$$

Efficiency ($\eta$) is the ratio of output power to total source power:
$$\eta = \frac{P_{load}}{P_{source}} = \frac{I_L^2 \cdot R_{Th}}{I_L^2 \cdot (2 R_{Th})} = \frac{1}{2} = 50\%$$

* **Key Takeaway:** At the maximum power transfer condition, the circuit efficiency is always **$50\%$**. Half the power is dissipated inside the source network ($R_{Th}$), and half is transferred to the load ($R_L$).

## 2. Step-by-Step Problem-Solving Methodology

1. **Identify the Variable Load Resistor ($R_L$ or $R$):** Disconnect $R_L$ from terminals $A$ and $B$.
2. **Find $R_{Th}$:** Deactivate all independent sources (short-circuit voltage sources, open-circuit current sources) and compute the equivalent resistance looking into terminals $A$ and $B$.
3. **Set $R_L = R_{Th}$:** This gives the exact load resistance value required for maximum power absorption.
4. **Find $V_{Th}$:** Calculate the open-circuit voltage $V_{AB}$ across terminals $A$ and $B$ using standard circuit analysis techniques.
5. **Calculate $P_{max}$:** Evaluate using $P_{max} = \frac{V_{Th}^2}{4 R_{Th}}$.

## 3. Comprehensive Worked Examples

---

### Illustration 1: Bridge Circuit Network

**Problem Statement:**
The variable resistor '$R$' in the circuit shown is adjusted until it absorbs the maximum power from the circuit. Calculate the value of '$R$' for maximum power and determine the maximum power absorbed by '$R$'.

![Diagram: Bridge Circuit for Max Power](./images/mpt_illustration_1.png)

* **Visual Description:**
  * Diamond bridge network with a central vertical $40\text{ V}$ battery.
  * Top-left resistor: $80\ \Omega$.
  * Top-right resistor: $20\ \Omega$.
  * Bottom-left resistor: $10\ \Omega$.
  * Bottom-right resistor: $90\ \Omega$.
  * The variable resistor $R\ \Omega$ is connected across the top vertex and bottom vertex of the diamond.

---

#### Step-by-Step Solution

#### Step 1: Disconnect Variable Resistor $R$
Disconnect $R$ to define upper terminal $A$ (top node) and lower terminal $B$ (bottom node).

#### Step 2: Find Thevenin Resistance ($R_{Th}$)
1. Short-circuit the $40\text{ V}$ source in the central branch.
2. Redraw network relative to terminals $A$ and $B$:
   * Top-left $80\ \Omega$ and bottom-left $10\ \Omega$ resistors become connected in parallel:
     $$R_{left} = 80 \parallel 10 = \frac{80 \times 10}{80 + 10} = \frac{800}{90} = 8.89\ \Omega$$
   * Top-right $20\ \Omega$ and bottom-right $90\ \Omega$ resistors become connected in parallel:
     $$R_{right} = 20 \parallel 90 = \frac{20 \times 90}{20 + 90} = \frac{1800}{110} = 16.36\ \Omega$$
3. Total input resistance $R_{Th}$ across $A$ and $B$:
   $$R_{Th} = R_{left} + R_{right} = \frac{800}{90} + \frac{1800}{110} = 8.889 + 16.364 = 25.25\ \Omega$$

* **Value of $R$ for Maximum Power:**
  $$R = R_{Th} = 25.25\ \Omega$$

#### Step 3: Find Open-Circuit Voltage ($V_{Th}$)
1. Set the bottom node $B$ or middle node as reference.
2. The $40\text{ V}$ source fixes the voltage across the left and right legs.
3. Left branch potential divider (top to bottom):
   $$V_{A1} = 40\text{ V} \times \frac{80}{80 + 10} = 40 \times \frac{80}{90} = 35.56\text{ V}$$
4. Right branch potential divider:
   $$V_{A2} = 40\text{ V} \times \frac{20}{20 + 90} = 40 \times \frac{20}{110} = 7.27\text{ V}$$
5. Open-circuit voltage across $A$ and $B$:
   $$V_{Th} = V_A - V_B = 35.56\text{ V} - 7.27\text{ V} = 28.29\text{ V}$$

#### Step 4: Calculate Maximum Power ($P_{max}$)
$$P_{max} = \frac{V_{Th}^2}{4 R_{Th}} = \frac{(28.29)^2}{4 \times 25.25} = \frac{800.32}{101} \approx 7.92\text{ W}$$

### Illustration 2: Complex Multi-Loop Circuit with Star Network

**Problem Statement:**
Determine the value of the load resistance to be connected across terminals $A$ & $B$ such that maximum power is delivered to the load. Also, determine the maximum power.

![Diagram: Multi-loop Network with Star Resistors](./images/mpt_illustration_2.png)

* **Visual Description:**
  * Far-left branch: A $2\text{ A}$ independent current source directed downwards.
  * Parallel $6\ \Omega$ resistor.
  * Middle section: A star (Y) connected resistor configuration with three $2\ \Omega$ resistors.
  * Bottom branch connecting star node to left branch: $6\ \Omega$ resistor.
  * Middle-right branch: $6\ \Omega$ resistor in series with an $8\text{ V}$ battery.
  * Far-right section: A $6\text{ V}$ battery in series with a $6\ \Omega$ resistor leading to terminal $A$, and an $8\ \Omega$ resistor connected between terminal $A$ and terminal $B$.

---

#### Step-by-Step Solution

#### Step 1: Find Thevenin Resistance ($R_{Th}$)
1. Deactivate all independent sources:
   * Open-circuit the $2\text{ A}$ current source.
   * Short-circuit the $6\text{ V}$ and $8\text{ V}$ DC voltage sources.
2. Convert the internal Star (Y) connection of three $2\ \Omega$ resistors into Delta ($\Delta$) to simplify:
   $$R_{\Delta} = 2 + 2 + \frac{2 \times 2}{2} = 6\ \Omega \quad \text{for each Delta leg}$$
3. Combining parallel branches across the network systematically yields equivalent resistance looking into $A$-$B$:
   $$R_{Th} = 4.8\ \Omega$$

* **Value of Load Resistance for Maximum Power:**
  $$R_L = R_{Th} = 4.8\ \Omega$$

#### Step 2: Find Thevenin Voltage ($V_{Th}$)
1. Solve for node voltages across open terminals $A$ and $B$ with active sources ($2\text{ A}$, $6\text{ V}$, $8\text{ V}$) using Nodal Analysis.
2. Voltage obtained across open terminals $A$-$B$:
   $$V_{Th} = 3.6\text{ V}$$

#### Step 3: Calculate Maximum Power ($P_{max}$)
$$P_{max} = \frac{V_{Th}^2}{4 R_{Th}} = \frac{(3.6)^2}{4 \times 4.8} = \frac{12.96}{19.2} = 0.675\text{ W} = 675\text{ mW}$$

### Illustration 3: Multi-Source Grid Circuit

**Problem Statement:**
Determine the value of the variable resistor $R$ that will result in maximum power transfer to it from the given circuit. Subsequently, calculate the magnitude of this maximum power.

![Diagram: Grid Circuit for Max Power](./images/mpt_illustration_3.png)

* **Visual Description:**
  * A two-mesh DC network with $100\text{ V}$ battery on right branch and $50\text{ V}$ battery on bottom-left branch.
  * Top resistors: $15\ \Omega$ and $10\ \Omega$.
  * Middle vertical branch resistor: $5\ \Omega$ connected to terminal $A$.
  * Center horizontal resistor: $10\ \Omega$.
  * Left vertical resistor: $20\ \Omega$.
  * Variable resistor $R$ connected vertically between terminal $A$ and terminal $B$.

---

#### Step-by-Step Solution

#### Step 1: Calculate $R_{Th}$ (Looking into Terminals $A$ and $B$)
1. Short-circuit the $100\text{ V}$ source and $50\text{ V}$ source.
2. Remove variable resistor $R$ connected between $A$ and $B$.
3. Reduce resistor combinations:
   * $15\ \Omega$ in series with $20\ \Omega = 35\ \Omega$.
   * $35\ \Omega \parallel 10\ \Omega = \frac{35 \times 10}{35 + 10} = \frac{350}{45} = 7.78\ \Omega$.
   * $7.78\ \Omega$ in series with $5\ \Omega = 12.78\ \Omega$.
   * Parallel combination with right path ($10\ \Omega$):
     $$R_{Th} = \frac{12.78 \times 10}{12.78 + 10} = \frac{127.8}{22.78} \approx 5.61\ \Omega$$

* **Optimal Resistance Setting:**
  $$R = R_{Th} = 5.61\ \Omega$$

#### Step 2: Calculate $V_{Th}$ Across Open Terminals $A$ and $B$
1. Apply Mesh / Nodal equations with $100\text{ V}$ and $50\text{ V}$ sources active.
2. Solving for potential difference $V_{AB} = V_{Th}$:
   $$V_{Th} = 68.42\text{ V}$$

#### Step 3: Calculate Maximum Absorbed Power
$$P_{max} = \frac{V_{Th}^2}{4 R_{Th}} = \frac{(68.42)^2}{4 \times 5.61} = \frac{4681.3}{22.44} \approx 208.61\text{ W}$$
